Notch

Privacy Policy

Last updated 26 November 2026

Training partners is available from version 1.2. If you are on an earlier version, the app has no sharing feature at all and nothing it holds ever leaves your iPhone.

Notch keeps your training on your iPhone. There is no Notch account, no Notch server, and nothing is sent to the developer — not your training, not your weight, not your food, not your photos. The developer cannot see any of it.

One feature is different, and it is off until you turn it on: training partners. If you switch it on and invite someone, the sessions you finish are copied to your own iCloud, where only the people you have invited can read them. Apple stores them for you; they are not sent to the developer, who cannot read them either.

If you never turn that on, nothing leaves your iPhone.

What the app stores on your device

All of this lives in the app’s own storage on your iPhone:

  • Your training history — routines, exercises, sets, weights, reps, reps-in-reserve, session dates and durations
  • Your body measurements — the ones you type in yourself
  • Your progress photos — if you choose to take any
  • Your settings — nutrition phase, target rate, theme and units

If you delete Notch, iOS deletes all of it with the app.

Training partners

Off by default. Nothing in this section happens unless you turn on Share my training in Setup and invite someone.

  • What a partner can read. For every session you finish after you turn sharing on: the date, how long it took, the routine’s name, and for each exercise the weight, the reps and the reps in reserve. They also see the name on your Apple ID, because that is how iCloud identifies you to someone you share with.
  • What a partner can never read. Your body weight, your measurements, your progress photos, your food and nutrition, and anything else in the app. None of it is included in what is shared, and none of it is sent anywhere.
  • They can read your whole shared history. Someone you invite today can read every session published since you first turned sharing on — not only the ones from after they joined.
  • It runs one way. Inviting someone lets them read your training. It does not let you read theirs: they have to invite you separately. Neither of you can see the other by acting alone.
  • They can only read. A partner cannot change, add to or delete anything of yours.
  • Where it is stored. In your own iCloud account, in a private area belonging to this app, using Apple’s CloudKit sharing. Apple’s handling of it is covered by Apple’s privacy policy. The developer has no access to it — data in a user’s private CloudKit database is not visible to the developer.
  • Turning it off, and removing people. Removing a partner takes your training off their device, including sessions they have already seen. Turning Share my training off ends every arrangement at once and does the same for everyone. Neither is a request to them — their copy goes with their access.
  • Names you give people. If you rename a partner inside Notch, that name is stored only on your iPhone. They never see it and it is never sent anywhere.

Apple Health

Notch asks your permission to read the following from Apple Health, so it can show your weight trend and your nutrition alongside your training:

  • Body weight, body fat percentage and lean body mass
  • Dietary energy, protein, carbohydrate and fat
  • Heart rate, used only to shade the training calendar by how hard a session was
  • Date of birth, used only to estimate your maximum heart rate
  • Workouts

It writes one kind of data back to Apple Health: completed strength workouts, so your training shows up in your activity rings and in other apps you use.

Granting this access is optional. Notch works without it — the screens that would show Health data display clearly-labelled example figures instead, behind a banner saying so. You can change or revoke access at any time in the Health app, under Sharing → Apps.

Health data read by Notch is used only to show you your own information inside the app. It is never used for advertising, never sold, never shared with anyone, and never included in what a training partner can see.

Camera

Notch asks for camera access only when you choose to take a progress photo. Photos are stored inside the app on your device. They are not uploaded, not backed up to any service by the app, not shared with anyone, and never included in what a training partner can see.

What Notch does not do

  • No Notch account, sign-up or login
  • No Notch server — the only thing the app ever talks to is your own iCloud, and only for training partners
  • No analytics, crash reporting or usage tracking of any kind
  • No advertising and no advertising identifiers
  • No third-party SDKs — none at all
  • No data sold or shared with anyone, for any purpose, beyond the partners you invite yourself

Children

Notch is a strength-training app intended for general audiences. It does not knowingly collect information from anyone, including children. Training partners requires an iCloud account and a deliberate invitation; nothing is shared without one.

Your rights

Your training is on your iPhone, and — only if you turned on training partners — in your own iCloud account. Nothing is held by the developer, so there is nothing for the developer to disclose, correct or delete.

  • To see exactly what a partner can read, open Setup → Training partners.
  • To stop sharing, turn off Share my training, or remove individual partners. Their copies go with their access.
  • To export your data, use your iPhone’s own backup (iCloud Backup or a computer backup)
  • To delete it, delete the app — iOS removes all of its data with it. Turn off sharing first if you want to withdraw what partners already have.
  • To stop Health access, revoke it in the Health app under Sharing → Apps

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the date at the top will change with it. If a future version of Notch ever sent data anywhere it does not today, this policy would be updated before that version was released, and the app would ask your permission first.
